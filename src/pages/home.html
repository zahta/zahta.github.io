The thread through the work

From mathematical structure to systems that must work with real inputs.

Mathematics trained me to make assumptions explicit and reason carefully about structure. In graph-learning research, that perspective became a way to represent complex relationships and test what a model learns. In applied computer vision, the challenge shifted toward turning imperfect images into measurements with checks around the model output. My LLM Systems Lab work brings the same scrutiny to retrieval: what evidence was found, what was missed, and how can a change be evaluated?

These are different technical settings, connected by a habit of treating validation, failure analysis, and clear limits as part of the implementation—not as a final presentation layer.

Engineering approach

A model output is a hypothesis to check.

In the vision workflow, checks for view, confidence, and anatomical plausibility sit alongside pose, segmentation, and geometry-based processing. In LLM Systems Lab, labeled supporting facts, retrieval metrics, regression-oriented tests, and traces make retrieval behavior easier to inspect. Different methods, same concern: make quality and failure visible enough to reason about.

Technical judgment

Model quality, robustness, inference speed, and implementation complexity interact. I aim to make those trade-offs explicit, test components independently, and describe limitations without stretching the evidence.
