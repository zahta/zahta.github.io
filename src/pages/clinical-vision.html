Confidential industrial work

From mobile images to structured musculoskeletal measurements.

A multi-view computer-vision workflow for a production-facing musculoskeletal-assessment application. Company identity, data, and proprietary implementation details are intentionally omitted.

Role
AI Developer · sole AI engineer on the workflow
Context
Remote · early-stage Germany-based medical startup
Dates
February 2025 – January 2026

Problem

The product needed to turn user-captured mobile images from multiple views into structured anatomical measurements for musculoskeletal assessment. Inputs were imperfect, and model outputs needed to be checked for reliability before the AI workflow could support product-facing features.

Contribution

Zahra owned the AI workflow from problem formulation and experimentation through model development, validation, and product integration. She worked with physicians, orthopedists, and sports-medicine experts to translate clinical and product requirements into AI features, measurement logic, validation rules, and outputs supporting personalized corrective-exercise recommendations.

She worked with the FastAPI-based backend and Swagger/OpenAPI interfaces to test and validate AI API workflows, collaborating with the software engineer responsible for backend and server deployment.

System components

The CV identifies these components in the pipeline; the view below groups them for clarity and does not claim the confidential production-stage order.

  1. Input viewsUser-captured mobile images from multiple body views.
  2. Visual analysisHuman detection, pose estimation, segmentation, and landmark localization/refinement.
  3. Quality controlView classification, confidence estimation, and anatomical plausibility checks for imperfect inputs.
  4. Geometric processingGeometry-based post-processing to produce structured anatomical measurements.
  5. Product integrationValidation of AI API workflows through the FastAPI/Swagger interface and coordination with the backend engineer.

CV-listed technologies include Python, PyTorch, OpenCV, RTMPose, DensePose, SAM2, and MediaPipe. The diagrammatic grouping is a high-level summary of supplied CV facts, not a disclosure of proprietary architecture.

Engineering judgment

The CV describes evaluating trade-offs among model accuracy, inference reliability, processing speed, and pipeline complexity. Reliability was supported through detection, view, confidence, and plausibility checks rather than treating raw model output as a final measurement.

The work involved translating domain-expert requirements into validation criteria and structured outputs while integrating multiple open-source computer-vision components.
